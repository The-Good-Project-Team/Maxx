How to read the bar

One line: who you are, then your three walls — chat, session, week — then where you're working. Each wall reads x/y. y is the standard — muted, fixed, never moving. x is where you actually are, and it is the only thing that carries colour.

@you · opus │ ctx 26/35% · 11 turns │ session 34/22% · 1h57m │ week 16/8% · 6d │ Maxx · main · 590e │ /maxx

 

Anatomy

Every piece, and what it is telling you.

  • x/y — session 34/22% reads "34% spent, against a standard of 22%". y is muted, fixed, and says nothing: it is the reference, and it stays quieter than the reading so it never steals the glance. x carries the whole verdict in its colour, so a glance at three pairs tells you where you stand three times without reading a word.
  • the four states — green while you have real room (under three quarters of the standard), plain ink as you close on it, amber once you're past, and red with a squiggle at the hard wall — the way a spell-checker tells you something is wrong. Only the session is ever bold below the wall: two shouts on one line is no hierarchy at all.
  • the trailing fact — each wall ends with what it is measured against: the chat has turns, the session has a clock, the week has days.
  • ctx — how full this conversation is, and how many turns filled it. Its line is whichever arrives first: 75% of the window, or 350k tokens — on a 1M window that's 35%. Past it, hand off deliberately with /fenix rather than letting auto-compact pick the cut for you.
  • session — your 5-hour window. Its line is your weekly share expressed in this window's terms. Pass it and nothing is denied; the rest of the week just gets less.
  • week — the only wall that can actually stop you. Its line is simply how far into the week you are: burn evenly and used tracks elapsed. Without it, week 16% is unreadable — excellent on Tuesday, alarming an hour in.
  • who and where — @you · model · repo · branch · id, trailing. The numbers lead, because they're what you glance at; this is what you look up once you know the numbers are fine. @you links to your dash — cmd-click it.
  • narrow terminal — pieces drop by rank, least useful first: the id, then the repo, the branch, the model, then each wall's trailing fact. Tighter still and whole walls go — where, then week, then session — because a cut number is not a smaller truth, it's a wrong one. @you, the chat wall and the mark never drop.
  • the wall — hit it and maxx hands you Switzerland in 8K with the reset clock. Contemplate.
